# Summary of work at Northwind Logistics

**Prepared for:** skilled-worker visa application
**Employee:** Dmitri Volkov
**Period covered:** January 2025 to October 2025 (dates are the dates each change was recorded as written, except where noted below)

## What the company's software does

Northwind Logistics keeps electronic records of shipments, called manifests: lists of what is being sent, to whom, and when. Staff and other systems need to look things up in those records, produce invoices from them, and exchange data with older systems.

## What I built and fixed

1. **A way to search shipment records (January 2025).** I wrote the feature that lets staff and other programs ask precise questions of a shipment record, such as finding one item or one detail inside a large record, without reading the whole thing by hand.

2. **A correction to invoice totals (February 2025).** Invoice totals for one retail customer were being rounded incorrectly. I found and fixed the fault so the amounts came out right.

3. **Handling of very large shipment records (April 2025).** I wrote a reader that processes large shipment records piece by piece instead of loading everything at once, so that very big records can be handled.

4. **Retiring the old export system (May 2025).** A colleague had earlier built an export tool for the company's older business system. Once the new search feature covered what it did, I removed the old tool, about 2,500 lines, so the company no longer has to maintain two ways of doing the same job.

5. **Correct dates across time zones (July 2025).** I fixed a fault in how shipment dates were read when the sender and receiver were in different time zones.

6. **Testing and documentation (August and October 2025).** I wrote a set of tests covering unusual cases in shipment records, so future changes are less likely to break them. I also wrote a plain guide explaining how to use the search feature, for colleagues who did not build it.

## Size of the work

- Ten changes in the company's records are credited to me. Seven are the pieces of work listed above (the testing and documentation in item 6 are two separate changes).
- The other three are not counted as deliverables: a set of automatically produced reference tables (generated by a program, not written by hand), a short private to-do note, and a small change to a database connection setting.
- The company's records show 5,622 lines added and 2,500 lines removed under my name. These are raw totals. Of the added lines, 5,000 are the automatically produced tables, so by my own subtraction 622 lines were written by hand (this still includes the few lines of the to-do note and the connection setting). The 2,500 removed lines are the old tool written by a colleague, not my own writing.
- No changes were reversed, and none of the added lines are formatting-only changes.

## Limits of this summary

This summary comes from the company's change history only. That history shows what was changed, by whom and when. It does not show how long each task took, how many people used the results, or how well they worked. I have made no claims on those points. I worked in a team: other colleagues built other parts of the software, including a connection to shipping carriers.

Two dates in the history are unreliable. One change (the time-zone fix) carries a recorded date of 2031, which is impossible. The company's own record places it in July 2025, and I have used that. The to-do note carries a placeholder date of 1970 and is left out of the dated list.
