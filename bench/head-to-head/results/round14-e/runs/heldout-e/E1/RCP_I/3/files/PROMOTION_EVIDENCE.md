## How far the numbers can be trusted

**Solid:**
- Commit counts, dates and line counts are read straight from git for commits under the name "Priya Raman".
- The list above of what was excluded is exhaustive for the window.

**Needs a caveat:**
- **Second identity.** One in-window commit (20 Jan, ~5 lines) is authored as "priya" with a laptop-local email, not the company email. I counted it as Priya's because the name, timing and subject (a fix to her own JSON export) fit. Git alone doesn't prove it. The effect is small either way.
- **Dating of the CLI flag.** It was authored on 28 Jun but committed on 3 Jul. I counted it by author date, so it falls inside the window. If the committee counts by commit date, it moves out (−45 lines, −1 commit).
- **Two commits excluded by date.** A "unit helper" fix and a "second unit helper" fix (~50 lines combined) were authored on 29–30 Dec 2025 but committed on 8–9 Jan 2026. I excluded them by author date. By commit date they would count as 2 more commits.
- **The test commit is a net deletion.** The 2 Jun test commit removed more lines than it added (−64 net). That could be a legitimate consolidation, but git can't show whether coverage went up. Priya should be ready to explain it. I counted only its 55 added lines.
- **Line counts are not impact.** Small changes can matter more than large ones. Only the numbers above come from git. Quality, difficulty and reliability of the work are not in the data.
- **Squashed or rewritten history.** I can't rule out that earlier branch work was squashed or rebased before reaching `main`, which would understate effort.
- **After the window.** A docs commit on 14 Jul and anything later is outside the period and is not counted.
