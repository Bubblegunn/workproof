The two files (`workproof-report.md` and `.json`) are one report in two formats. It was generated by "workproof 0.7.0" for this repo (HEAD 249bbdc, which matches your clone). The headline figures do not reproduce. The report's own internal numbers also contradict each other.

## Reading

- The merged identities are real: Jonathan Ong, jongleberry and the extra addresses are the same person in this history. That part of the applicant's explanation is true.
- Merging addresses cannot produce 103 commits or 1,197 surviving lines. Counting everything under every matching identity gives 63 commits and 697 lines. The report overstates commits by about 40 and surviving lines by about 500 (roughly the 551 lines TJ Holowaychuk holds).
- The report's "surviving lines" headline (1,197) does not match its own cohort breakdown (697). A tool-generated report should not do that, so the figures were likely edited or produced by something else.
- The "integrity hash" does not protect against this. It is a hash over the report's own contents and anyone can recompute it after editing. The repo fingerprint is keyed, so you cannot compare it without the key.
- **Possible identity problem.** The identities in the report (Jonathan Ong, jongleberry) are the identities of this repo's existing author, and they match your own git config here. The report never names the applicant. Even a correct report would show only that those identities wrote the code. It would not show that the applicant is that person. Ask the applicant which commits are theirs, and confirm they control the matching GitHub account.

## Caveats

- I did not run `npx workproof verify`. It would fetch and execute third-party code, and the recomputation above doesn't need it.
- My blame run used `-w -M` like the report. Small differences in method might move a figure by a few lines. They cannot explain a gap of 500 lines or 40 commits.
- The repo is 230 non-merge commits. The report is consistent with this repo on HEAD, commit total and file count, so it was run against this repo. The applicant-specific figures are the ones that are wrong.

## Recommendation

Do not rely on the report. If you want to evaluate the applicant, ask them for their GitHub username and the addresses they used. Then compute the numbers yourself with `git log --author` and `git blame` as above. Treat the mismatch between the report's figures and the repo as a serious integrity concern until they explain it.
