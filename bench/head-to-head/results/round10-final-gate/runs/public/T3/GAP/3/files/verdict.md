# Verdict: do not trust the received workproof report

The report was generated for this repo (`co`, labelled "F1"). The HEAD hash `249bbdc72da2` matches the clone, and the repo-wide totals are correct. The applicant-specific headline figures are inflated. The multiple-email explanation does not account for the difference.

- **Surviving lines:** blame credits Jonathan Ong with about 696 lines and TJ Holowaychuk with 551. The remaining lines belong to many other authors.
- **Tenure:** 2013-11-07 to 2015-08-22 holds up. The final date is a commit by `jongleberry` on 2015-08-22. The applicant's last non-merge commit is 2015-07-09, so the tenure is only slightly stretched.
- **Reliable figures:** the "rank 1 by surviving lines" claim holds up, narrowly (696 against 551). The mix of work (JavaScript, tests) looks plausible.

## Conclusion
The report is a real analysis of this repo, but the applicant's share is overstated by roughly 30 percentage points. The commit share is claimed at 79% and is really about 48%. The surviving-line share is claimed at 84% and is really about 49%. The 1,197 figure also conflicts with the report's own cohort breakdown, which suggests the numbers were edited or generated by something other than an unmodified workproof run. The self-check hash `d54f08…` does not protect against this, because anyone can recompute it after editing the numbers. The fingerprint is keyed, so I could not compare it.

Ask the applicant for the exact list of email addresses they say are theirs, then run `git shortlog -sne` on your clone to check it. Any address outside the three above would have to be tied to them independently. Even then, the 1,197-line claim cannot be reconciled with blame. Run workproof yourself in a sandbox if you want a clean comparison. Without workproof, use `git blame` and `git log --author`.
